# Depth
Build depth with multiple cues:
overlap; foreshortening; relative scale; perspective convergence; spatial planes; atmospheric perspective; contrast and saturation falloff; detail reduction; softer/lost edges; selective DOF.

## Atmospheric perspective
With distance, forms tend to become less clear, less saturated, lower contrast, less detailed and more grouped/simplified.

## Spatial planes
Extreme foreground → foreground → subject/middle ground → background → far background.

## Overlap
Avoid near-touching objects and edge tangencies that flatten space. Use clear overlaps to state front/back relationships.

## Detail hierarchy
High-contrast texture tends to advance; low-contrast/simplified texture tends to recede.

## DOF
Use DOF as a hierarchy/storytelling tool, not as a generic photorealism effect. Architectural hero views may benefit more from deep focus plus atmospheric separation than aggressive blur.


## Expanded book-grounded diagnosis

Source keys L, C and P and page-number conventions are defined in the [evidence register](book-evidence-register.md). ArchViz applications are production interpretations, not software instructions from the books.

### Depth exists inside objects as well as between planes

P 147-170 discusses texture, value, edges, overlap and temperature as cues within forms. P 154 particularly connects texture contrast with recession.

**ArchViz application:** examine a single facade's recesses, returns and material transitions before adding atmospheric haze to the whole image. Distinguish missing geometric depth from missing visual evidence of that depth.

### Recheck depth after lighting and detail changes

P 247 and 253-254 connects later treatment with spatial clarity and material cues.

**ArchViz application:** revisit overlap, scale anchors and plane separation after material refinement. Rain streaks, joints and grain should follow the surface; excessive distant detail can compete with the intended spatial hierarchy.

## Scope of depth heuristics
Atmospheric falloff depends on the medium, lighting and view; the tendencies above are not mandatory per-object adjustments. Overlap establishes ordering without proving metric distance. Quiet or decorative spatial treatment can be intentional. Separate focus blur from participating media and preserve required architectural information.
