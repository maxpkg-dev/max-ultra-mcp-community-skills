# Perspective & Camera
## Horizon / eye level
Treat the horizon as the viewer's eye level and the fundamental reference for grounded perspective.

## Families
One-point: frontal/axial controlled views.
Two-point: natural corner views with moderate distortion.
Three-point: vertical convergence, low/high POV and drama.
Multi-point/curvilinear: strong distortion; use deliberately.
Bird's-eye and worm's-eye views strongly affect scale and hierarchy.

## Diagnosis
Identify eye level, camera height, tilt, dominant vanishing directions, vertical behavior, foreground distance and perspective exaggeration before changing focal length.

## ArchViz rules
A low camera or converging verticals are not automatically errors if intentional. Consider focal length and camera distance together. Use facade, road, curb, paving, terrain and landscape directions as perspective cues. Verify scale with people, doors, cars, furniture and trees.


## Expanded book-grounded diagnosis

Source keys L, C and P and page-number conventions are defined in the [evidence register](book-evidence-register.md). ArchViz applications are production interpretations, not software instructions from the books.

### Repeated elements establish scale and perspective

P 198, 240 and 243-245 uses recognizable and repeated dimensions to support spatial interpretation.

**ArchViz application:** check doors, steps, rails, paving and windows as related scale cues. RailClone repetitions should remain consistent with the design's dimensions and orientation; coherent spacing often contributes more to believable scale than additional surface detail.

### A wide view is a camera decision, not an automatic defect

P 186 and 195-196 addresses distortion and viewing geometry.

**ArchViz application:** evaluate camera position, field of view, crop and edge placement together. Compare alternatives when furniture or facade features stretch near the frame edge. Do not turn a textbook cone-of-vision example into a universal lens limit; focal-length comparisons also need sensor or film-gate context.

### Horizon comparisons require compatible subjects

P 204 uses standing figures as scale references. C 623 offers a related drawing shortcut.

**ArchViz application:** compare figures with attention to their height, pose and ground elevation. Do not assume that the horizon must cross every person's eyes: children, stairs, slopes and different body heights invalidate that shortcut.

### Multiple object orientations imply multiple direction sets

P 219-220 and 239 discusses differently oriented forms.

**ArchViz application:** avoid forcing rotated buildings or furniture into the main facade's vanishing directions. Use consistent three-dimensional geometry as the reference; composition adjustments must preserve the intended design.
