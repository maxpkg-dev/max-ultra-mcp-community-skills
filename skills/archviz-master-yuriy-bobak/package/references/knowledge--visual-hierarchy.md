# Visual Hierarchy
Design three reads: primary, secondary, tertiary.

Attention increases with strong value contrast, saturation, sharp edges, high-frequency texture, isolation, scale, directional lines, highlights and recognizable figures.

Do not let bright sky, white cars, high-contrast trees, random people, specular pavement, signage, windows or foreground grass accidentally overpower architecture.

Diagnostic: list the first five noticed elements in order and compare with intended hierarchy.


## Expanded book-grounded diagnosis

Source keys L, C and P and page-number conventions are defined in the [evidence register](book-evidence-register.md). ArchViz applications are production interpretations, not software instructions from the books.

### Reduce a competitor before intensifying the subject

C 293-296 describes several contrast channels; C 332-335 shows the benefit of reducing a competing element's prominence.

**ArchViz application:** assess value, hue, saturation, edge sharpness and texture contrast separately. A foreground tree may need quieter highlights or less textural contrast, rather than a brighter building. Allow different elements to use different contrast channels.

**Diagnostic:** name the first, second and third reads and explain which visual differences produce that order.

### Edge hierarchy is a compositional control

C 347 and 626 discusses the balance of lost and found edges.

**ArchViz application:** use overlap, tonal relationships, lighting and atmosphere to control boundary visibility. Preserve boundaries needed to understand architecture. Do not make every edge equally prominent, and do not translate "lost edge" into indiscriminate post-production blur.

### Variation should serve the composition

C 508 discusses repeated natural shapes and visual sameness.

**ArchViz application:** evaluate ForestPack clusters in the chosen camera: silhouette, spacing, overlap and contrast matter alongside species and random seeds. Preserve intentional planting patterns. Do not randomize engineered facade repetition simply because organic arrangements benefit from variation.

### Separate material value from lighting value

P 211-213 explicitly distinguishes local values and illumination.

**ArchViz application:** use controlled comparison views or appropriate render diagnostics to determine whether a wall appears too dark because of its material or its illumination. Avoid compensating for a lighting problem by making the material implausibly bright.
