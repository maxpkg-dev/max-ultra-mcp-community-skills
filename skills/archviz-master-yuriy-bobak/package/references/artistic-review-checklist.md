# Artistic Review Checklist

Evaluate against the actual brief; quiet, low-contrast and centered compositions may be intentional.

- Intent: name the intended situation, first read and required architectural information.
- Camera and scale: check eye level, view geometry, familiar dimensions and the design's actual rotations; do not force all objects into one direction set.
- Masses: inspect projected silhouettes and large value groups. Detail should not unintentionally fragment them.
- Contrast: evaluate value, hue, saturation, shape, edge and texture separately. Grayscale cannot diagnose every competing cue.
- Light: identify direct, environmental and bounced contributions. Diagnose shadow color from the surrounding illumination, not a warm/cool formula.
- Materials: check illumination, reflected context, texture scale and directional structure before changing material parameters.
- Depth: distinguish overlap, plane cues, scale, focus blur and participating media; preserve legibility rather than maximizing haze.
- Narrative: poses, gaze, props and viewer role should support the situation. Test the most plausible unintended reading.
- Delivery: review full frame, small view and intended resolution; for multiple views, recheck every required camera. For animation, also check continuity through motion.

Record observed evidence, uncertain causes, the smallest useful next test and unresolved tradeoffs. Do not claim render or scene verification from a written concept.
