# Light
Light describes form, establishes hierarchy, separates planes, creates mood and reveals materials.

Distinguish direct/source light, ambient/environment light, bounced/reflected light and rim effects.

## Shadows
Form shadow occurs on the object's own surface. Cast shadow projects onto another surface.
Evaluate direction, edge softness, shape, source logic and compositional role.

Distant sunlight behaves approximately as parallel directional light; nearby artificial sources produce more radial shadow behavior.

Shadow shapes can frame subjects, lead the eye, divide surfaces, create rhythm and separate depth planes.

## ArchViz checks
Where is the brightest important region? Do facade planes read volumetrically? Are entrances/features receiving useful light? Are shadows helping composition or creating clutter?


## Expanded book-grounded diagnosis

Source keys L, C and P and page-number conventions are defined in the [evidence register](book-evidence-register.md). ArchViz applications are production interpretations, not software instructions from the books.

### Warm light does not require cool shadows

L 107-114, particularly 111-112, explains shadow appearance through the illumination that reaches shadowed surfaces. The surrounding sky, ground and nearby objects matter.

**ArchViz application:** when a shadow looks wrong, inspect environmental illumination, bounce sources, local material color and exposure before applying a blue tint. A warm courtyard can produce warm indirect illumination under a warm key light.

**Diagnostic:** identify what the shadowed surface faces and which sources it can receive. The skill should ask this explicitly.

### Shadow softness and shading gradients need different diagnoses

L 93-94 discusses penumbra; L 99-100 distinguishes illumination changes associated with distance from changes associated with form.

**ArchViz application:** distinguish source extent, source/receiver geometry, surface orientation and light falloff. Do not prescribe image blur for every harsh shadow, or modify roughness to repair a lighting gradient.
